# Admin Dashboard Redesign - Design Specification

**Date:** 2026-07-14
**Branch at specification time:** `codex/orders-page-redesign`
**Scope:** Admin dashboard frontend, dashboard API, shared reporting calculations, tests, and English/Arabic translations.

## 1. Purpose

The dashboard answers one question: **How is the restaurant doing right now?**

It is not another Daily Reports page and not another Orders History page. It shows the
current business day, explains meaningful changes, and sends the owner to the correct
admin page when action or detail is needed.

Design principles:

- Start with today's story, then show evidence.
- Prefer four trusted numbers over many loosely related cards.
- Show exceptions only when they deserve attention.
- Keep finance definitions consistent with Daily Reports.
- Use natural English and Arabic. Avoid inflated analytics language.
- Make the first view useful on a 1024px POS screen.

## 2. Locked product decisions

| Decision | Choice |
|---|---|
| Time scope | Current business day only; no date picker |
| Main comparison | Today so far versus the average of the previous four matching weekdays, cut at the same elapsed business time |
| Headline metrics | Sales today, Orders, Average check, Estimated close |
| Sales definition | Paid sales including tax minus refunds issued during the current business day |
| Main chart | Cumulative today versus a typical matching weekday, hour by hour |
| Tables | Conditional `Tables right now` block; hidden when Tables is disabled |
| Products | Top five today, ranked by net sales; units and matching-weekday comparison shown |
| Payments | Compact proportional display; no doughnut chart |
| Refunds, voids, discounts | Exception-only; full detail remains in Daily Reports |
| Register differences | Exception-only; link to Shifts |
| Recent orders | Omitted; Orders History already owns order lookup and printing |
| Tax and profit | Omitted from headline analysis; tax remains in Daily Reports and profit is unsupported without expenses |
| Narrative | Deterministic translated templates; never generated by AI |
| Numerals | Latin digits in both languages; preserve the existing currency presentation |

## 3. Information architecture

The reading order is fixed:

1. Business date, last refresh time, and one plain-language summary sentence.
2. One connected four-metric ledger.
3. `Needs attention`, only when at least one actionable item exists.
4. Cumulative pace chart and, when enabled, `Tables right now`.
5. `What sold` and `Cash and card`.

There is no oversized hero, greeting, decorative statistic, recent-orders feed, or
generic insight carousel.

### 3.1 Top summary

The header shows the current business day and last successful refresh. The summary is
one sentence built from response reason codes and translated frontend templates, for
example:

> Sales are 12% ahead of a typical Tuesday. More orders caused most of the increase.

The four metrics share one visually connected surface:

- **Sales today:** `sales_collected` for the current business-day window.
- **Orders:** paid orders invoiced in that window. A later refund does not erase the
  fact that the order was handled.
- **Average check:** paid sales processed before refund events, divided by paid order
  count. This matches the Daily Reports definition and prevents an old-order refund
  from distorting today's average basket size.
- **Estimated close:** forecast full-day `sales_collected`, or absent when confidence
  gates fail. Absence is preferable to a placeholder or false estimate.

### 3.2 Needs attention

This block is absent when no action is required. It may contain:

- unusually high refund value/rate;
- unusually high void value/rate;
- unusually high discount value/rate;
- closed-register cash differences;
- low-stock products when stock tracking is enabled.

Each row states the fact, includes the useful amount/count, and links directly to Daily
Reports, Shifts, or Inventory. Example: `One closed register is 3.50 JD short.`

Warnings are deterministic. Refund, void, and discount warnings require all of:

1. at least three comparable historical weekdays;
2. a non-trivial absolute value (initial floor: 5.00 JD);
3. a rate above its normal floor (refund/void: 5%; discount: 10%); and
4. a rate at least twice the matching-weekday rate.

Rates use:

- refund rate = refunds issued / sales processed;
- void rate = void value / (sales processed + void value);
- discount rate = discounts / (sales processed + discounts).

Zero denominators produce no analytic warning. Closed shifts warn when their stored,
rounded variance is non-zero. Low stock uses `stock_enabled` and the configured
`low_stock_threshold`; it does not invent a second threshold.

### 3.3 Pace chart

The chart replaces the current 14-day trend. It contains:

- a strong solid line for today's cumulative `sales_collected`;
- a quiet dashed line for the average matching weekday;
- hourly business-time points plus the current partial-hour endpoint;
- direct legend labels and concise currency tooltips.

Refund events are placed at refund time, so a cumulative line may fall. Historical
trend exploration remains in Daily Reports.

### 3.4 Tables right now

This block is returned and rendered only when `tables_enabled = 1`. It shows:

- occupied physical table count;
- open unpaid value, counted once per distinct open order;
- longest-open table and elapsed time.

Merged child tables must not duplicate open order value. The longest-open calculation
uses the open unpaid order's creation time. A direct link opens the Tables screen.

### 3.5 What sold

Show the top five products for the current business day, ranked by net sales value.
Each row includes:

- full product name, without API truncation;
- net sales including tax and current-day refund events;
- net units (sold minus returned);
- percentage difference from that product's average across eligible matching days,
  when history exists.

Product calculations reuse the same line-discount, prorated order-discount, service
charge exclusion, and refund-event attribution rules as Daily Reports Sales Details.
Do not create a simplified `quantity * price` dashboard calculation. The section links
to the sales breakdown for detail.

### 3.6 Cash and card

Use a thin horizontal proportion bar followed by ledger rows. Each active payment
method shows amount and share. Values are net collected tender amounts after refund
allocation and reconcile with the dashboard sales figure. Do not use a doughnut chart.

## 4. Comparison and forecast rules

### 4.1 Eligible matching days

The backend examines the previous four calendar dates with the same weekday as the
current business date. A day is eligible when it contains evidence the restaurant was
operating (a paid order, refund, or void event). Comparisons require at least three
eligible days. Otherwise the dashboard still shows today's facts but omits comparisons,
product deltas, narrative claims about pace, and the forecast.

All same-time comparisons use elapsed minutes from the configured business-day start,
not wall-clock date boundaries. This preserves correctness for overnight business days.

### 4.2 Typical values

For each metric, `typical` is the arithmetic mean across eligible matching days. The
same-time baseline cuts every historical day at today's elapsed business minute. Full
day values are used only by the closing forecast.

Percent changes are omitted when the typical denominator is zero. Display values use
money rounding only after aggregation.

### 4.3 Estimated close

For each eligible historical day:

`progress share = sales collected by current elapsed minute / full-day sales collected`

The estimate is:

`max(today sales collected, today sales collected / average progress share)`

It is hidden when any of these gates fail:

- fewer than three eligible matching days;
- fewer than two elapsed business hours;
- fewer than five paid orders today;
- average historical progress share below 15%;
- today sales are not positive;
- any required denominator is zero; or
- full-day historical sales are too inconsistent (coefficient of variation above 50%).

The UI does not display a confidence percentage. It simply omits an unreliable number.

### 4.4 Narrative selection

The API returns facts and reason codes, not prose. The frontend selects a translated
template:

- within 5%: close to typical;
- above 5%: ahead;
- below -5%: behind;
- unavailable baseline: neutral current-day sentence.

Order-count and average-check percentage changes identify the likely driver. A driver
must exceed the other change by at least 3 percentage points; otherwise the sentence
mentions both or makes no cause claim. Templates accept structured parameters only.
There is no LLM call, free-form concatenation, or English sentence returned by the API.

## 5. Visual system

The dashboard belongs to the same visual family as the redesigned Daily Reports:

- warm neutral page background;
- flat surfaces and thin dividers;
- restrained teal/brand accent;
- no gradients, glass effects, heavy shadows, or card mosaic;
- Space Grotesk for English and IBM Plex Sans Arabic;
- sentence-case natural headings: `Sales today`, `Needs attention`, `Tables right now`,
  `What sold`, and `Cash and card`;
- tabular numerals for changing financial values;
- amber/red reserved for genuine attention states.

The high-taste skills influence typography, spacing, hierarchy, consistency, and
anti-slop review only. Landing-page AIDA structure, giant section spacing, random
layout, pinned scroll effects, and GSAP spectacle are explicitly rejected for this
operational dashboard.

Motion is limited to short opacity/transform state changes and hover/focus feedback.
It respects `prefers-reduced-motion` and never delays access to data.

## 6. Responsive and bidirectional behavior

- **Wide desktop:** four metrics in one row; chart takes roughly two thirds and Tables
  one third; lower sections form a balanced two-column row.
- **1024px POS screen:** headline metrics form a compact 2x2 grid; chart becomes full
  width; Tables moves below. Summary and headline numbers remain in the first view.
- **Mobile:** single-column priority order, 2x2 metrics, shorter chart, no page-level
  horizontal scrolling.

The Arabic layout mirrors structural alignment. The chart's chronological x-axis stays
left-to-right. Use an explicit Latin numbering system for dates, times, percentages,
counts, and money while preserving the existing JD currency style. Every new visible
string must exist in both English and Arabic dictionaries.

Keyboard focus is visible. Interactive rows and icon actions have accessible names and
at least 44px touch targets. Chart meaning is also available as text/current values so
color and pointer tooltips are not the only access path.

## 7. API contract

`GET /api/admin/dashboard` remains current-day only and accepts no historical date
selector. The response becomes presentation-neutral:

```text
business_date, as_of, refreshed_at
history: eligible_days, comparison_ready
headline: sales_today, orders, average_check, estimated_close
comparison: typical values, amount/percent deltas, pace state, driver reason
pace: today cumulative points, typical cumulative points
tables: null or enabled/current operational values
attention: typed items with message key, parameters, severity, and destination
products: id/name/net_sales/net_units/delta
payments: method/amount/share
```

The existing 30-second server cache may remain, but its payload must be tied to the
current business date and current elapsed-time bucket so a business-day rollover cannot
serve stale data. Queries that do not depend on one another run in parallel.

Finance and product aggregation should be extracted from or delegated to shared report
services. Dashboard code must not copy a competing version of Daily Reports SQL.

## 8. Vue structure and lifecycle

Refactor the page toward Vue 3 Composition API with small, purposeful boundaries:

- dashboard data composable: fetch, refresh timer, abort, stale-data retention;
- summary/metric ledger;
- pace chart;
- attention list;
- tables block;
- product list;
- payment split.

Do not split one-line decorative wrappers into components. Derived values use computed
state instead of template method calls with hidden side effects.

Because the admin router uses `KeepAlive`, refresh starts on activation, pauses on
deactivation, and all timers/requests/listeners are cleaned up on unmount. A request
sequence or abort guard prevents an older response from replacing a newer one.

## 9. Page states

- **Initial loading:** geometry-matched skeleton; no content jump.
- **No sales yet:** natural empty sentence; Tables and attention may still render.
- **Sparse history:** current facts render; comparison and estimate are absent.
- **Refresh failure with prior data:** retain last successful values and show a quiet
  retry notice.
- **Initial failure:** focused error state with retry; no fabricated zero dashboard.
- **Optional feature absent:** omit the block rather than render an empty card.

## 10. Verification

Backend integration/unit tests cover:

- business days crossing midnight and exact exclusive range bounds;
- previous four matching weekdays and same-elapsed-time cutoffs;
- insufficient history and zero denominators;
- refund timing, old-order refunds, partial refunds, and split-tender allocation;
- prorated line/order discounts in product values;
- forecast formula and every confidence gate;
- merged-table deduplication, open value, and longest-open table;
- warning rate/amount thresholds and register variance;
- cache rollover and payload contract.

Frontend tests and visual review cover:

- every narrative reason code in English and Arabic;
- Latin digits with unchanged currency formatting;
- feature-gated Tables/stock blocks;
- loading, empty, sparse-history, stale-data, and error states;
- refresh cleanup under `KeepAlive` and stale-response protection;
- no Vue missing-property or lifecycle warnings;
- keyboard/touch access and reduced motion;
- LTR/RTL at wide desktop, 1024px, and mobile widths.

## 11. Non-goals

- Historical date browsing.
- Recent order lookup, invoice preview, reprinting, or A4/thermal printing.
- Full refund/void/discount ledgers.
- Tax filing detail.
- Profit, margin, food-cost, or expense analysis.
- AI-written recommendations or anomaly detection.
- Changes to checkout, refund processing, table operations, or register closing logic.
